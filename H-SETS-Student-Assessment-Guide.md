# H-SETS — How your work is assessed

Use this guide with your module workbook, the [project handbook](H-SETS-Portfolio-Projects.md) and the [competency gates](H-SETS-Competency-Gates-Student.md). Your instructor supplies assessment dates and feedback. A practice example is not an assessed answer or evidence that you completed a lab.

## What counts towards completion

| Component | Course weight | What you submit |
|---|---:|---|
| Module knowledge questions and written scenarios | 15% | Your answers and reasoning |
| Weekly practical work | 20% | Your actual lab tests, explanation and required reflection |
| Individual gates G1–G4 | 20% | A fresh practical case and individual explanation |
| Projects P01–P07, equally weighted | 30% | Each project's required evidence and defence |
| P08 capstone and defence | 15% | The final investigation and its required evidence |

You need **at least 70% overall**, all four gates passed, and all eight projects passed. Each gate requires at least 70/100 and its critical requirements. Each project requires at least 70/100, all its critical acceptance tests and at least 6/10 in the individual defence. A high overall average cannot compensate for a missing gate or failed critical project test.

## Practice, assignments and marks

Instructor demonstrations, the guided practice questions in the notes, and practice checkpoints are for learning. They are not additional graded assignments. The two named lesson assignments in each module supply the knowledge/scenario and weekly practical evidence categories above. Their feedback is formative: you use it to improve. That does not mean the submitted assignment is excluded from the course grade.

Workbooks retain their existing raw marks. A lesson may have 30, 50 or 100 total points because its rubric differs. A larger raw total does not make that module more important. The instructor converts each module's knowledge/scenario score and practical score separately to percentages, then averages modules equally within each of those two categories. Where the workbook awards reflection marks, attach them to the practical category; do not create a sixth course category. Keep the original rubric totals and show the calculation on feedback.

For example, if a module awards 40 knowledge/scenario points and you earn 32, that category score is `32 ÷ 40 × 100 = 80%`. If its practical total is 60 and you earn 42, that practical score is `42 ÷ 60 × 100 = 70%`. Do not merge these into a single percentage and use it for both categories. These numbers illustrate calculation only; use your workbook's actual totals.

For an overall example, knowledge 80%, labs 75%, gates 80%, P01–P07 average 75%, and P08 80% produce:

```text
(80 × 0.15) + (75 × 0.20) + (80 × 0.20) + (75 × 0.30) + (80 × 0.15)
= 77.5%
```

Completion still depends on every gate and project meeting its individual pass conditions. Your instructor must show the gate aggregation used in the gradebook; each gate's own pass requirement remains mandatory.

## What evidence is enough?

Submit the items named in your workbook and project brief. For a test, show the requirement, identity/system, expected result, action, actual result and evidence reference. Add a short sentence explaining the result. A settings screenshot supports configuration evidence; a functional test shows behaviour. Extra screenshots do not automatically earn extra marks.

| Recorded status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pass | You ran the test and its observed result met the requirement |
| Fail | You ran the test and its observed result did not meet the requirement |
| Not run | You did not execute it; state the missing prerequisite or other reason |
| Unverified | Available evidence does not establish whether the requirement was met |
| Not applicable | The requirement does not apply to the assigned case; give the reason and confirm it with the instructor |

A denied operation can be a passed test when denial was the requirement. Missing equipment is not a student technical mistake: report it so the instructor can restore access and arrange the work. It also cannot be recorded as a passed practical.

## Feedback and another attempt

Feedback should identify the missing concept or test, explain the next practice action, and give the reassessment arrangements. Reassessment uses a different but equivalent case. Keep both attempts and label the latest assessed result. Critical requirements stay the same. Ask for clarification if you cannot tell which evidence supports a mark.

You may use approved notes and documentation. Disclose help, including AI assistance, and be able to explain every submitted action. Do not submit another person's evidence as your own. Request accessibility or communication support early; the instructor can adapt how you demonstrate a skill while retaining its technical requirement.
