# H-SETS — 18-Week Module Plan

[Course home](README.md) · [Detailed curriculum](COURSE_CURRICULUM.md)

The course contains **18 weekly modules and 40 detailed lessons**. Follow module order. Lesson numbers remain stable and retain the adopted content and sequence; a module groups related lessons for one teaching week. Modules 1–3 develop foundations, and Module 4 introduces virtualisation.

## How to use a teaching week

Begin with the module overview and preparation notes. Read the linked lessons in order, work through their examples, complete the assigned practice and keep the lesson assessment questions and take-home tasks. End by explaining the evidence in the module checkpoint. The same evidence may support a lesson task and a project where the project permits it; a weekly checkpoint does not create a separate scoring category.

Modules 1, 2, 3 and 17 contain three lessons. All other modules contain two. Reading length and practical difficulty vary, so a lesson count does not imply equal workload. Instructors should allocate guided sessions and independent study after timing the actual material with beginners. This plan does not claim that the earlier course's hours or grading scheme still apply.

| Projects 08 and 09 | Plan with the integrated skills through Week 17 | Their original multi-week work and acceptance tests |
| [Module 18 independent document investigation](modules/Module-18/INDEPENDENT-INVESTIGATION.md) | Week 18, after studying the Lesson 40 worked case | Supplied Cedar evidence analysis and its assessment rubric; live tool competence remains separate |
| Project 10: Northstar Manufacturing | Week 18 or later, after all required prior competence | Its original 3–4 week engagement and acceptance tests |

The 18 weeks specify the **teaching and note sequence**. They do not compress ten substantial portfolio projects into short classroom exercises. Project work progresses alongside the lessons where prerequisites allow, and remaining integration work needs scheduled completion time after teaching. Finishing Week 18 does not by itself mean every project is complete. Agree the remaining project dates from the briefs and actual learner progress; no fixed extra duration has been established by this adaptation.

Keep the full practical requirements, including recovery, allowed/denied testing, troubleshooting and reporting. Use staged guests or instructor-hosted services where the learner computer cannot run the whole range. Keep an unperformed task marked as not run. Neither this timetable nor the reference's estimates substitute for an instructor pilot.
