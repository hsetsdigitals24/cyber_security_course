## How the course fits together

A **module** is one teaching week's group of topics. A **lesson** is a full chapter within that module. There are eighteen modules and forty lessons. A **tool lab** develops practical operation of a tool or system; a **portfolio project** applies several skills to a larger problem. Starting a project stage does not mean the entire project is complete.

## Follow the same route every week

| Step | Page | Your action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Module start | Read the overview, preparation and learning outcomes. |
| 2 | Student notes | Open the lessons in order and study their explanations and examples. |
| 3 | Practice and assessment | Use the direct links to exercises, questions and take-home tasks. |
| 4 | Completion checklist | Review evidence, record pending work and move to the next module. |

The module pages organise existing lesson requirements. They do not add a second assessment or change the original scoring. Read the linked assignment for its exact instructions. Your instructor provides the submission destination and deadlines.

## Before the first week

Be comfortable typing, creating folders, saving and reopening files, and taking a screenshot without exposing personal information. Use the [computer-skills bridge](COMPUTER-SKILLS-BRIDGE.md) if you need practice. Check the [system requirements](README.md#minimum-system-requirements) before the VM work in Week 4.

Weeks 1–3 use explanations and supplied evidence. A tool command appearing in the notes is not an instruction to test a public or personal system. Use the assigned environment and only the live steps for which setup and permission are established.

## Keep work easy to review

Create a folder for each module, for example `H-SETS/Module-01/`. Use clear filenames such as `L01-risk-table.md` and `L02-threat-analysis.md`. A folder named after your module is for your work; do not overwrite the course notes.

Every evidence record should identify the module, lesson/task, date, action, observed result and interpretation. Add a screenshot or output when it supports the claim. Explain what it demonstrates instead of submitting an unexplained image. Record remaining questions and next actions. Do not include passwords, recovery keys or real personal records.

Keep a simple task record:

| Task | Status | Evidence or reason | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson question | Complete / in progress | Your response filename | Revise if needed |
| Live exercise | Complete / not run / deferred | Test evidence or missing prerequisite | Prepare the required environment |

## When you get stuck

Before a live practical, use the [assigned lab setup sheet](practicals/templates/CLASS-LAB-SHEET.md) to record the systems, inputs and recovery route provided by your instructor.

Record the exact step and message. Check the lesson's prerequisites, expected output and troubleshooting guidance. Avoid changing several settings at once. If the next action would exceed the assigned scope or risk losing access, give the instructor your task reference, observation and checks already performed.

## Completion and feedback rules

Keep separate records for reading, lesson submissions, live tool competence and portfolio projects. Reading a chapter or viewing an instructor demonstration does not complete a live requirement. Mark work complete only when its required evidence and acceptance checks have been reviewed; mark unavailable activities pending with a return date.

The required programme comprises the assigned forty-lesson work, seven tool-lab pathways and ten project briefs. Follow the phased weekly plan; optional challenge briefs and additional tool packs are not extra requirements unless assigned. Use each assessment's published rubric. The independent Cedar assessment has a 70/100 threshold; that threshold must not be assumed for other assessments.

Before the cohort begins, the instructor must issue a dated completion plan giving: required submission IDs, private submission destination, deadlines including post-Week-18 project work, pass thresholds for each graded assessment, practical acceptance criteria, feedback dates, resubmission opportunities and the person approving completion. Until this plan is issued, the cohort schedule and overall award decision are not final. Do not invent dates from the eighteen-week reading sequence.

After feedback, revise the identified sections, retain the original attempt and submit a change note showing what you corrected and how you verified it. Record the reassessment outcome. Unperformed live work remains pending even when a document assessment passes. Course completion requires all required categories to be accepted under the issued plan; it is not a guarantee of employment.
