# H-SETS Cybersecurity — Career Development Plan

Use this during M18 and the completion studio. It supports honest role targeting; it does not promise employment.

## Target role and evidence

Target one primary entry-level role: junior SOC/security operations, vulnerability-management support, IAM support, or systems/network support with security duties. Record five genuine vacancies available when the activity is performed. For each, save the URL, employer, date checked, location/work arrangement, core tasks, required skills and experience language. Do not treat one vacancy as proof of the whole market.

## Thirty-day plan

Correct weak project evidence, repeat one failed or uncertain practical, polish two case studies, create a role-focused CV, and practise explaining a technical decision in plain language. Apply only to roles whose essential conditions you can meet or reasonably develop into; record applications and feedback.

## Sixty-day plan

Build one bounded extension relevant to the target role, such as another detection test set, a different Linux service baseline, an additional packet case or an access-review variation. Do not begin a large new tool collection while core evidence remains weak. Repeat a mock interview with new questions.

## Ninety-day plan

Review application outcomes, interview gaps and technical feedback. Update portfolio evidence, repeat the vacancy sample, refine the role target and choose the next recognised learning or certification objective only after checking current official objectives, cost and relevance.

## Mock technical interview guide

The interviewer should select unfamiliar variations and ask for evidence rather than trivia:

1. Walk through a network failure from symptom to verified service recovery.
2. Show how you proved an unauthorised identity was denied without breaking authorised access.
3. Which raw event supports this alert, and what uncertainty remains?
4. How did you distinguish a scanner claim from a validated vulnerability?
5. What changed after remediation, and how did you check normal function?
6. What would you do if monitoring stopped during an investigation?
7. Which incident observation changed your hypothesis?
8. Which response action required approval, and what business effect could it cause?
9. What did your recovery test prove and fail to prove?
10. Identify one claim in your portfolio that you would narrow after reviewing the evidence.

Score clarity, technical reasoning, evidence, honesty about limitations and appropriate escalation. A polished answer without supporting work is weaker than a careful, traceable answer that acknowledges an unknown.
